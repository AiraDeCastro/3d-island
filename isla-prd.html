01

Overview & vision

Isla is a single-scene, browser-based 3D experience: one island, rendered in three.js, that the visitor can reach into. Drag a finger or cursor through the sand and it displaces — footprints, trenches, trails that soften back to smooth over time. Waves roll in on their own rhythm and darken the sand where they reach, then retreat leaving a foam line. Coconut trees lean and hiss in a wind that also tugs at hut thatch and hammock cloth. Nothing needs to be clicked to start; the island is already alive when the page loads, and touching it is the whole point.

The reference feeling is a real beach at low tide — the odd calm of a place that keeps moving on its own, and the small satisfaction of leaving a mark in the sand that the sea will eventually erase. Isla is not a game to win; it's an ambient object to visit, fidget with, and leave running in a tab.

02

Goals & non-goals

Goals

  • A WebGL scene that reads as beautiful within the first second — lighting, water, and foliage carry the page before any interaction happens.
  • Direct manipulation that feels physical: sand deforms under the cursor/finger with no visible lag, and the effect persists and decays like real sand.
  • An environment that feels inhabited even when idle — wind, waves, and light are always doing something.
  • Runs acceptably on a mid-range phone, not just a gaming desktop.

Non-goals (v1)

  • No scoring, quests, or win condition.
  • No multiplayer or shared state — each visitor gets their own island. (Candidate for backlog, see §5.)
  • No terrain/scene editor — visitors shape sand, they don't place objects or build.
  • No accounts, no monetization, no data collection beyond standard anonymous analytics.
03

Who it's for

Casual visitors arriving via a shared link or a portfolio — people who give a page 5–10 seconds to earn their attention and want something calming, not something to learn.

Designers and developers who'll inspect the craft — shader quality, load performance, and interaction feel are the pitch here, so the build itself is part of the audience's experience.

Anyone looking to unwind for a minute — a browser-tab equivalent of a desk fidget toy or a screensaver worth staring at.

Core features — MVP

Everything below ships together; Isla doesn't have a meaningful v1 with any one of these missing.

Feature manifest
FeatureWhat it doesPriority
Sand interaction Cursor/touch drag stamps into a height field in real time — displacement plus recomputed normals, so light catches the new shape immediately. Marks soften and level out over ~30–60s. Fast drags kick up a light burst of sand particles. P0
Ocean & shoreline Shader-driven waves (summed Gerstner functions) roll toward the beach on a loop. Where water meets sand: darkened "wet sand" band, a thin foam trail that advances and retreats, and light interaction with any footprints in the wash zone. P0
Coconut trees Hand-placed low-poly palms; trunk and frond vertices sway on a shared wind uniform, each tree offset by a random phase so the grove doesn't move in lockstep. P0
Beach huts One or two thatched huts with a cloth element each — a curtain, a hammock — driven by the same wind system, plus a warm interior glow toward dusk lighting. P0
Wind system One global direction/strength value driving tree sway, cloth motion, water ripple detail, and a light drift of loose sand particles — the thread that ties the scene together. P0
Sky & light Procedural sky with a positioned sun driving shadows and water specular highlights; ACES tone mapping so highlights on water don't blow out. P0
Camera Damped orbit/pan, clamped to a range that always keeps the island framed — no way to spin off into empty sky or clip through the sand. P0
Ambient audio Looping surf and wind bed, muted by default on mobile per autoplay policy, one visible mute toggle. P0

06

Interaction & UX principles

  • No chrome by default. Full-bleed canvas; the only persistent controls are mute and a quality toggle, both small and corner-docked.
  • Self-teaching, not explained. A soft "drag the sand" hint fades in after two idle seconds and fades out permanently after the first drag — no modal, no tutorial overlay.
  • Everything eases. Camera, sand relaxation, and wind gusts all move on damped curves; nothing snaps.
  • Same experience, adapted rendering. Touch and mouse both drive the sand and camera; on lower-tier devices the scene simplifies (see §7) rather than removing interactions.
Design bar: if a first-time visitor doesn't touch the sand within 10 seconds of arriving, the scene hasn't done its job of inviting them in.
07

Technical approach

Rendering

Three.js on a Vite/TypeScript build. Water is a sum of Gerstner waves computed per-vertex on a subdivided plane, with a normal map derived in-shader for lighting and a Fresnel term for the reflect/refract blend at grazing angles. Sand displacement uses a GPGPU-style ping-pong render target: pointer drags stamp a brush shape into a height texture each frame, that texture drives vertex displacement on the beach mesh and feeds a recomputed normal for lighting, and a slow decay pass blends the texture back toward flat over time — the same mechanism handles both the deformation and its erosion.

Foliage and cloth sway from a single wind uniform (direction, strength, gust noise) sampled in the vertex shader with a per-instance phase offset, so palms, thatch fringes, and hammocks all answer the same "weather" without separate systems.

Assets & performance

  • glTF models, Draco-compressed geometry, KTX2/Basis textures.
  • Instanced rendering for repeated foliage; frustum culling and simple LOD swaps at distance.
  • Adaptive quality: device capability probe on load sets shadow resolution, wave mesh density, and pixel ratio; a manual override sits in the quality toggle.
  • Post-processing kept light — bloom on water highlights and ACES tone mapping; ambient occlusion only at the highest quality tier.

Hosting

Static build on a CDN-backed host (Vercel/Netlify-class); no backend required for MVP since each session is a self-contained island.

Risks & open questions

Risks
RiskMitigation
Mobile GPU ceiling Wave shader + sand heightmap are the two most expensive systems. Mitigate with the adaptive quality tiers in §7 and a hard cap on wave-mesh subdivisions on mobile. High
Asset weight vs. load target Trees, huts, and textures compete with the <3s goal. Mitigate with Draco/KTX2 compression and loading a low-detail island first, then streaming detail in. Medium
Safari WebGL quirks Historically inconsistent shader precision and extension support. Mitigate with early cross-browser testing rather than a Chrome-only build cycle. Medium

Open questions

  • Is Isla a standalone product, a portfolio centerpiece, or a component meant to embed elsewhere (e.g., a landing-page hero)? Affects how much chrome and branding it should carry.
  • Is a shared/multiplayer island (§5) worth the infra investment, or does a private island per visitor stay the better fit for the "calm, yours alone" feeling?
  • Is "Isla" the final name and domain, or a working title?
